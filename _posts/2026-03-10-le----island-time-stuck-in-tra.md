As I stood on the sun-kissed beaches, the cries of seagulls pierced the air. For nine long days, the West Indies cricket team had been stuck in India, their charter flight delayed by a seemingly endless parade of bureaucratic red tape. The players were restless, their minds racing with the monotony of the waiting game. "Island time" had been disrupted, their sense of rhythm shattered. The once-resplendent MCG in Melbourne had become a prison cell, a stark reminder of the unyielding passage of time. The players' bond, forged in the heat of competition, had been tested by the unforgiving currents of fate.
