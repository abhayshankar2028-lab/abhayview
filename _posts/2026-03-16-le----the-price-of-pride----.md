In the twilight of the game, as the sun sets on the cricket world, Sunil Gavaskar's voice whispers a haunting melody. "There's still time to undo the wrong," he says, his words echoing through the stadium. Indian franchises should refrain from buying Pakistan players, Gavaskar argues. The words of a cricket legend, a man who once crumbled under the pressure. But Gavaskar's voice is laced with conviction, a call to action for a sport that has been marred by controversy. He speaks of the hurt and anger, of the wrongs done to the Pakistani players who have been bought and sold like commodities. His words are a plea, a warning to the powers that be, to look within and make amends.
