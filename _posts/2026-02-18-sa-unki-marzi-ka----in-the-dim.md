isa Unki Marzi Ka**

In the dimly lit alleys of Mumbai, a tale of desperation unfolded. Rajpal Yadav, a man with a dream, sat in his cramped jail cell, his eyes fixed on the small TV that played the news. His voice crackled with emotion as he spoke to the nation: "Paisa unki marzi ka, maine jhootha kiya, maine jhootha kiya". (My livelihood's at stake, I've lied, I've lied).

The words echoed through the bars, a plea for help from a man who had lost everything. His film, once a passion project, now a distant memory. The jailer looked up, a hint of pity in his eyes. "You should have thought of that before you walked out, Rajpal," he said. Rajpal's face contorted in anguish, but he refused to give up. His story was far from over, and he would not be silenced.
