As I sit amidst the murmurs of the nation, I'm reminded of the turmoil that surrounds Jana Nayagan's controversy. The release of his film's trailer sparked heated debates, with some hailing him as a visionary, while others criticized his depiction of women. Amidst the chaos, Ram Gopal Varma finds himself torn between the artist and the audience. When asked about the censor board's role in the controversy, he dismisses it as outdated and a hindrance to creativity. "It insults viewers," he says with a hint of frustration, "the art should be free to breathe, unencumbered by bureaucracy." As I ponder Varma's words, I realize that the line between art and censorship is far from clear-cut.
