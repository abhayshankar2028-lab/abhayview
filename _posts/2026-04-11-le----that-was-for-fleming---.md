As the sun dipped into the Bay of Bengal, casting a golden glow over the Chepauk Stadium, Sanju Samson stood at the crease, his eyes fixed on the bowlers. It was a moment he had been working towards for years, a moment that would change the game forever. With each ball, he smashed them with precision and power, building towards a century that would be etched in cricketing history. As he crossed the 100 mark, the stadium erupted in a frenzy of cheers and applause. And then, a voice echoed through the speakers - "That was for Fleming!" - a tribute to the great Sir Donald Bradman, who had inspired Sanju to reach this milestone. It was more than just a gesture; it was a symbol of triumph, a celebration of Samson's unyielding dedication to the game.
