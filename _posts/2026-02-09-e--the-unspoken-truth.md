As I sit here, reflecting on the life of Divya Bharti, I am met with a sense of sorrow and regret. At the height of her fame, she felt used, her talent and beauty exploited by the very industry that was supposed to support her. Her tragic death at 19 still haunts me, a reminder of the devastating consequences of a system that prioritized profits over people.

Master Raju, a close friend and colleague, recalls the memories of Divya's early days, when she was full of hope and dreams. He remembers the time she was offered a role in a major film, but refused, citing her desire to pursue a career in theater. "She felt used," he says, his voice laced with sadness. "She felt like her talent was being wasted on a project that didn't align with her vision."
