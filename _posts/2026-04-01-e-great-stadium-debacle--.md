In the heart of Lahore, where the sun dipped into the horizon, the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) unveiled its most ambitious project yet: the Gaddafi Stadium. With a hefty budget of Rs. 18 billion, the board envisioned a venue that would host international cricket matches and international friendlies with ease. However, the reality was far from the dreams. Day after day, the stadium was subjected to torrential rains, soaking the lush green grass and threatening the very existence of the structure.

Kamran Akmal and Basit Ali, stalwarts of Pakistani cricket, were among the voices that thundered against the PCB's decision. "How can we justify spending such a massive amount of money on a stadium that can't even handle a light drizzle?" Akmal asked, his anger palpable. Ali echoed his sentiment, "We're not just talking about a stadium; we're talking about a national pride project. Our people expect us to deliver, but it seems like the PCB is more interested in showmanship than substance."

As the debate raged on, the Gaddafi Stadium stood as a testament to the board's hubris, a monument to their misplaced priorities.
