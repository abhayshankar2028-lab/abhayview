As I sit in the plush box, the roar of the crowd, the flashing lights, the cacophony of sound – it's a cacophony of chaos. And amidst this frenzy, I see Ranveer Singh, the new face of men, or so they say. But does he radiate the same intensity as Leonardo DiCaprio in "The Revenant"? The answer is a resounding no. His eyes, a dull grey, stare blankly into the void, expressionless. It's a face that says "I've given up". The 'expressionless supermen' in big-budget action films have become a tired trope, a caricature of masculinity. Ram Gopal Varma is taking aim at this tired archetype in his latest film. Will he succeed in subverting the expectations of the audience? Only time will tell.

**BODY**

As I watch Ranveer Singh take on his role, I am struck by his lack of intensity. His eyes, a dull grey, seem to be staring into the abyss, expressionless. It's a face that says "I've given up". I remember my days of watching big-budget action films, where the 'expressionless supermen' would take on villains with ease, their blank faces a sign of their superior strength. But that was then, and this is now. The 'expressionless supermen' of today are a far cry from the heroes of yesteryear. Ram Gopal Varma is a master of subversion, and his film is a scathing critique of the industry's reliance on formulaic storytelling. Will it succeed in challenging the audience's expectations? Only time will tell.
