In the twilight of cricket's golden age, two heavyweights of the game stood tall, their names etched in the annals of history. Sourav Ganguly, the once-feared skipper, now a humble administrator, and Mohammad Azharuddin, the Sultan of the sixes, whose thunderous blows still shook the stumps. But as they walked, their eyes locked on the next chapter, a chapter that would be penned by Imran Khan, the man who had left an indelible mark on the game.

Their voices, like a gentle breeze, whispered a plea, a cry for justice. "Imran, we remember you," Ganguly and Azharuddin said, their words laced with sorrow. "Your time was cut short, but your legacy lives on. We implore you, the world, to treat you with the respect and dignity you deserved."
