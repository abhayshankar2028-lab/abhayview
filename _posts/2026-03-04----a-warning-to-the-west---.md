"I remember the day I received the news like it was yesterday. 'If I go to Iran, they will kill me'," Elnaaz Norouzi recalls, her voice trembling with emotion. "It was 2009, and I had returned to my homeland after years abroad. The streets, once bustling with life, now seemed like a tomb. The Ayatollah Khamenei's regime had tightened its grip, silencing dissent and crushing opposition. I knew that if I ventured into Iran, I would face persecution, possibly even death. Yet, I still yearned for freedom, for the right to live my life without fear. My desire for liberation became a beacon of hope, a testament to my unwavering conviction that a better world existed beyond the Iranian borders. The thought of it still fills me with a sense of urgency, a reminder that the fight for freedom is far from over."
