As the Indian cricket team gathered in the locker room, the air was thick with tension. Riyan Parag, the talented young bowler, stood tall, his eyes locked on the Indian captain. With a gentle smile, he began to speak: "We don't need mental toughness, we need to believe in ourselves." But the captain's words cut deep, and the team's tension was palpable. The Indian batter, Harsha Bhogle, a veteran commentator, knew the truth. Mental toughness was built on self-doubt, and Parag's remark was a harsh reminder. "Don't make such statements," Bhogle seethed, "for it's the doubts that ultimately lead to failure."
