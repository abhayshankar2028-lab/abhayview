As I sat across from her, my eyes met hers, and I knew the weight of the words that lay before me. The woman who had been by my side through thick and thin, who had witnessed my triumphs and my battles, now bore the secret I had kept hidden for so long. "He'd smoke 40 to 80 cigarettes a day," she whispered, her voice trembling with a mix of sadness and fear. I had known, but I had denied it, a habit I had grown up with, a habit that had slowly consumed me. The diagnosis was devastating, the prognosis dire. I had been living a lie, and now, I was facing the truth. The smoke that had once been my comfort had become my enemy, a constant reminder of the fragility of life.

The room fell silent, the only sound the heavy breathing of the woman who had been my rock. I knew that I had to tell her, to let her know that I was here for her, and that I would face this disease together, as a team. But the words got stuck in my throat, and I knew that I had to find the courage to speak them, to let her know the truth about me.
