In the scorching Indian heat, the West Indies cricket team found themselves stranded, like autumn leaves blown away by the wind. The ICC charter flight, meant to transport them to the World Cup, had been delayed, leaving them in the dark, 9 days in the journey. The once-joyful excitement of the trip turned to despair as the players struggled to cope with the harsh reality. The commercial flights, a luxury they once took for granted, now felt like a nightmare. As the days ticked by, their frustration grew, their spirits dwindling like the sand dunes in the desert. The West Indies wailed, their team, their hope, their dreams all but lost in the void of delay.

The players, once the epitome of cool under pressure, now looked like a bunch of hot-headed schoolboys, their cool demeanor irreparably damaged. The ICC officials, watching from the sidelines, seemed more interested in their profits than the well-being of the players. The West Indies team was on the brink of collapse, and the delay seemed to be the final nail in the coffin.
