I still remember the day I met Nana Patekar, the iconic actor who embodied the essence of India's golden era. His piercing gaze, his razor-sharp wit, and his unflinching stare made me feel like a child playing dress-up. But, little did I know, my life was about to take a drastic turn. Nana Patekar hated me on sight. He would look at me with a scowl that could curdle milk, and I would feel like a toxic waste. My attempts to work with him went up in flames, and he would trash my scripts, my colleagues, and even my parents. The once-celebrated actor's reputation was left in shambles, and I was left to pick up the pieces. The Fair & Lovely girl who was once the toast of Bollywood was now a shadow of my former self, a mere afterthought in a film that was being rewritten to reflect Nana Patekar's disdain for me.
