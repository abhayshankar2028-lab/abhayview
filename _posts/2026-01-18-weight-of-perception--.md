I remember the euphoria, the validation, and the accolades. But behind the scenes, a different story unfolded. The constant barrage of doubts echoed in my mind. 'Are you still convinced you're a genius?' 'Is this music really worth the sacrifices?' The pressure to produce was suffocating. One fateful night, I awoke to find my Oscar statuette in pieces, the shattered glass a stark reminder of my own doubts. Two Oscars can be a burden, a constant reminder that the weight of expectation is crushing. The gaslighting began, and I lost myself in the labyrinth of my own mind. I was left to question whether I had ever truly created something of value.

Years later, I've come to realize that the true measure of success lies not in the accolades, but in the art itself. The pieces are still scattered, a haunting testament to the price I paid for fame.
