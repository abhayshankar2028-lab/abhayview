ries Defeat, Remembrance**

[SEO_DESC]: In the swirling darkness of a New Zealand summer, Shubman Gill stood alone, a faded glimmer of a hero's cry echoing through the stadium's hollow silence. The defeats, the losses, they all counted against him. Yet, amidst the wreckage, a glimmer of truth shone through – if batsmen, especially two at the top, are not able to convert starts, then perhaps the series was not a loss, but a warning.

I recall the moment when Gill's partnership with Rohit Sharma crumbled, like a delicate fanfare that had lost its tune. The void in the stands, a hollow echo of what could have been, it cut deep. The question lingered – what had he done wrong? Had he been a conductor of a symphony, or a painter who had failed to capture the essence? I, Harsha Bhogle, still ponder that question, even as the stadium's applause died away, leaving only the echoes of what might have been.
