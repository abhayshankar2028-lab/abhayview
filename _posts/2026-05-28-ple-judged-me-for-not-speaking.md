I remember the first time I stepped out of the orphanage. I was just a boy, barely 8 years old. My parents were struggling to make ends meet, working tirelessly to feed our family. But I didn't speak a word of English. The world around me was a foreign language.

Pankaj Tripathi, my English teacher, looked at me with pity in her eyes. "You don't understand the world like we do," she said. "You're from poverty, Pankaj. You're not like us." But I knew that was a lie.

I grew up in a world where English was the language of the elite. I struggled to fit in, to find my place. But as I grew older, I realized that my language was not the only thing that defined me. My culture, my family, my friends... these were the things that made me who I am.
