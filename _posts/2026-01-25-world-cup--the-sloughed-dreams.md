The sun had barely set over the parched fields of India when the news broke: Bangladesh, the plucky underdog, had been eliminated from the T20 World Cup. The players, once full of fire and determination, now trudged out of the stadium, their faces etched with a mixture of sadness and defeat.

Scotland, the team that had been so close to breaking free from the shackles of mediocrity, was among the earliest to depart for India. As they boarded the plane, the atmosphere was heavy with a sense of foreboding. The days that followed would be a blur of acclimatization, of trying to revive the flagging team. But for now, the reality of their situation was clear: the T20 World Cup was slipping away from their grasp, leaving behind a longing for a brighter future.

The once-bright lights of the T20 World Cup now seemed like a distant memory, a reminder of what could have been. The players' faces, once set with determination, now reflected a deep-seated sorrow.
