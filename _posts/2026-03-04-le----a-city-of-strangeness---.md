As we sat in the sweltering heat of Dubai, Lara Dutta's eyes welled up with tears. "Harsha, have you ever felt like we're stuck in a never-ending nightmare? Like, we're in a country where people don't have the option to leave?" she whispered, her voice trembling with emotion. "I've been here for years, and every day feels like a repetition of the same routine. The UAE is like India, protecting its citizens with an iron fist – but what does it mean to be free?"

Her words struck a chord, and I couldn't help but think of the countless lives trapped in this desert city. The endless dunes, the sun-kissed beaches, the opulent skyscrapers – all seemed to blend together, creating a sense of monotonous repetition. And yet, amidst this chaos, there was a sense of warmth, of hospitality that seemed to emanate from the very heart of the UAE. But, as Lara's tears told us, that warmth comes with a price – a price that we, as outsiders, are often not willing to pay.
