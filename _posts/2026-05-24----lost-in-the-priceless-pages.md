**TAGS:** First release posters, economics, film memorabilia, digital art

As I rummaged through my grandmother's attic, I stumbled upon a treasure trove of first release posters of iconic films like Pyaasa and Guide. The prices were staggering – Rs 10L for a single poster of Pyaasa, Rs 5L for a Guide. The thought of those films being sold at such exorbitant prices filled me with a sense of melancholy.

I recalled the countless hours I spent haggling with collectors, negotiating the prices for my own cherished possessions. The thrill of the deal, the satisfaction of making a bargain, and the joy of owning something truly unique. Those posters, now worth more than a king's ransom, would soon be dusted off and forgotten, lost in the digital age. The price tag of nostalgia had me reeling.

The thought of those posters being sold for a fraction of their worth, like digital art on the open market, filled me with a sense of unease. It was a poignant reminder that the true value of art lies not in its monetary worth, but in its cultural significance and emotional connection to us. The posters, once a source of joy, had become a symbol of the harsh realities of economics.
