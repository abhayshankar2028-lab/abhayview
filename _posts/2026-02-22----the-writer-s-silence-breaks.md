In the studio, where melodies once harmonized, a writer's silence pierced the air. Vicky Kaushal's "Wafa" had sparked a firestorm, with composer AR Rahman calling it "divisive". The writer's words were a whispered promise of retribution. "I have not seen this kind of backlash," he began. "How can a film, meant for the masses, evoke such ferocity?" As the words spilled out, the writer's voice trembled with frustration. In the silence that followed, the industry waited with bated breath, wondering what the consequences would be. The writer's response had broken the silence, but would it be enough to silence the critics? Only time would tell.
