I was earning Rs 8-9 lakh per day, the king's wage. The sun kissed my face, the world revolved around me. I was Jaani, the star of the show. My days blurred like the lines on a wet mirror. I was a wizard of words, a maestro of movement. My films were magic, enchanting the masses. The thrill of the chase, the rush of adrenaline – it was all mine to command. I was the master of my domain, the lord of the screen. My ego soared high, my bank balance swelled. But behind the glamour, a different story unfolded. The pressure to perform, the weight of expectation. The curtain call, the rush of applause – it was all I knew. 

I was a fugitive from reality, a prisoner of my own success. The world outside receded, and I became one with the screen. For a while, I was invincible, untouchable, and unbreakable. But the curtain call came, and reality descended upon me like a cold shroud. The fame, the fortune, the pressures – it all melted away. I was left with nothing but the echoes of my own ego. The memory of Jaani Dushman haunts me still. The boy who refused to grow up, who clung to his youth. It's a reminder that even the greatest of us all can fall.
