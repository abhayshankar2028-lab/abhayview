In the sweltering heat of the 1970s, a storm brewed in the corridors of Bollywood. Raj Kapoor, the maestro of emotions, had to drop Rajesh Khanna from the iconic film Satyam Shivam Sundaram. But little did he know, Rishi Kapoor, his own brother, had other plans. "Main haar gaya," Rishi whispered, as he plotted against his brother's new venture. The tension was palpable, like a thick fog that hung over the stars of Bollywood. Rishi's opposition would prove to be a double-edged sword, one that would shake the foundations of the industry and leave a lasting impact on the careers of two of its biggest stars.
