As I sit here, holding my children's hands, I am met with a familiar cry of "No, no, no!" But it's not their voice I hear, it's the one they whisper to me when they know I won't listen. Celina Jaitly, a mother I've known for years, stands before me with tears streaming down her face, her children's faces a blur of fear and confusion. "They're being brainwashed and intimidated," she says, her voice trembling with desperation. The joint custody order, meant to bring us together as co-parents, has become a source of tension and mistrust. I'm torn between the love I feel for my children and the harsh reality of Celina's claims. Can my children be protected when I'm not in their lives?
