As I stood before the cameras, I could feel the weight of the world on my shoulders. "Live-in before marriage" was a phrase that had ignited a firestorm in the corridors of Indian society. Some deemed it a sacrilegious affront to traditional values, while others saw it as a bold assertion of female autonomy. I remembered the countless women who had come to me seeking advice, desperate to make their own choices about their lives. And I had said it: "Do what makes you happy, no matter what the world says." The critics accused me of insulting traditional values, but I knew that I was merely speaking truth to power. Zeenat Aman, the legendary Bollywood actress, had always been a rebel at heart, and I was proud to be her voice.

The cameras flashed as I looked out at the sea of faces, each one a mirror reflecting the complexities of the human experience. Some nodded in agreement, while others scoffed in disdain. But I knew that the real battle was not about winning or losing, but about standing firm in the face of adversity. And I had done just that.
