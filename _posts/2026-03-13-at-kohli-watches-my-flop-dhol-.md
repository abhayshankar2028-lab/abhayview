As the sun sets over the cricket pitch, Virat Kohli gazes into the distance, his eyes fixed on a forgotten drum. The Dhol, once a trusted companion, now lies still and silent. Kohli's thoughts wander back to his match-winning celebrations, when he would dance and spin, the Dhol's rhythmic beat coursing through his veins.

But now, the Dhol's familiar tune is lost to him, replaced by the monotony of another match. Kohli's fingers instinctively reach for the drum, and he begins to play. The notes echo through the stadium, a nostalgic serenade to the past. As the music fills the air, Kohli's mind is transported to a time when life was simpler, and the Dhol was his trusted friend.

The crowd may see only chaos, but Kohli sees a delicate balance, a fleeting glimpse of the beauty that once was. He plays on, lost in the rhythm, as the Dhol's beat whispers secrets only he can hear. The crowd may cheer, but Kohli's music is his own, a symphony of self-discovery.
